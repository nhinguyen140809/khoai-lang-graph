Search the web and academic papers for documents in which real people speak about the topic of the study. Returns the title, the address, the publisher and the start of the text of each page.

How to search well:
- Write queries like a search engine query: three to eight keywords that name the people and the situation, such as `students study room booking complaints`. One idea per query; do not paste the plan.
- Search for voices, not for rules. Pages that an organization wrote about itself (policies, how-to guides, opening hours, FAQ, product pages) hold no stakeholder voice. Add words that people use when they talk: complaints, frustrated, survey results, interview, review, experience, forum.
- Look for different kinds of documents: studies and papers, surveys, interviews, forum threads and community discussions, complaint or review records, news reporting. Name the kind in `source_type` so that the search adds the right words.
- Aim at the places where people talk with `allowed_domains`. A plain query returns mostly pages that organizations wrote about themselves (vendors, policies, guides), so for a forum thread, a complaint record or a piece of news, name the site: `reddit.com`, `quora.com`, `stackexchange.com`, `news.ycombinator.com`, `trustpilot.com` or the site of a student or local newspaper. For a study or a survey, name a research site such as `arxiv.org`, `researchgate.net` or `sciencedirect.com`. Pass one to three domains for one idea, and search the plain web for the same idea only when the named sites return nothing.
- Use `blocked_domains` to drop a site that keeps returning noise, and `news` for recent events.
- Cover every stakeholder type of the plan, with at least one query for each.
- Read what came back before the next query. If the results are thin or off topic, change the angle (other words, another kind, another place) instead of repeating the same query.
- Stop when the plan is covered by documents of several kinds. You have at most six rounds.
